# Brand, campaign and retention decisions

The intended recognition device is consistent: HISAAB DO masthead, warm paper, carbon ink, one violet accent, a visible receipt. The design borrows newspaper organisation without copying any publication's logo or masthead.

## Campaign system

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Core palette | Newsprint `#f2eddf`, ink `#252219`, restrained violet from the product theme |
| Night palette | Warm charcoal `#14131b`, readable cream `#f5efe2`, violet accent |
| Cover type | Strong editorial serif for Latin headlines; readable native Devanagari for Hindi |
| Body type | Clear sans serif, plain sentence case, generous line spacing |
| Signature composition | Masthead, one idea, one illustration or true screenshot, short invitation |
| Illustration | Original linocut/halftone table scenes, ledgers, receipts, pens and hands |
| Excluded imagery | Party/state emblems, politicians' caricatures, nationalistic competition cues, fabricated certificates or official seals |
| Public CTA | “Play a round” / “एक राउंड खेलिए” |
| Sound/motion | Optional, restrained, reduced-motion support; no flashing urgency |

The generated poster is the launch hero. Keep a 5% safe margin when adapting it for feeds. Its 4:5 composition suits an editorial post. For a 9:16 story, commission an intentional adaptation rather than cropping away the masthead or receipt. Screenshots need real readable product content, not invented metrics. A carousel can show one question, the submitted answer, then the receipt; label it a walkthrough so viewers do not confuse it with a live timed round.

## Retention hypotheses and limits

| Player need | Product choice | Evidence to collect | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competence | Clear feedback, readable receipts, visible permanent progress | Wrong-answer recovery and voluntary next round | No loss of earned XP for absence |
| Autonomy | Solo or social modes, chosen themes, easy quit | Mode choice and successful exits | No forced invite or notification opt-in |
| Relatedness | Familiar circles and per-circle nicknames | Invite-to-completed-game and circle return | Private links, no fake presence or spam |
| Recognition | Fresh competition windows and earned honours | Eligible participation and fairness reports | No payment advantage, cash prizes or humiliation |
| Curiosity | A source attached to the answer | Receipt and source opens | Avoid concealment of material rules |

The hidden honour can be a pleasant discovery; its eligibility, active period and expiry must be understandable once earned. Public marketing must preserve the surprise. It is an overlay on permanent progress, never evidence that a person is more patriotic or politically correct. Do not infer politics from rankings or target ads based on answers.

## Finite specialist panel

These are AI role-based reviews, not independent human experts or user research. They use the product brief and the cited literature as constraints.

### Round 1: proposed product and brand decisions

- Marketing lens: start with the receipt and a playable round, because the proposition is specific enough to demonstrate. Treat civic and quiz communities as pilot recruitment ideas rather than claimed demand.
- Motivation lens: invite voluntary mastery and belonging. A source helps an incorrect answer remain useful. A daily reset should offer a fresh start without destroying earned progress.
- Visual lens: one masthead and a consistent paper/ink palette produce recognisable assets. The art should show shared curiosity, not factional victory.
- Senior UX/technical lens: public promises must match the server state machine and identity model. “Instant”, “fair” and “account” need exact qualifications. A tiny latency difference must not be marketed as a measured human skill difference.

### Round 2: challenge and resolution

- Challenge: the hidden title could sound like a political merit badge. Resolution: keep it a satirical, temporary game honour with no political targeting, and omit its name from public marketing.
- Challenge: social proof is absent. Resolution: use the real playable product and receipts as proof, never fake testimonials or player counts.
- Challenge: game-like ads could confuse players or obstruct evidence. Resolution: propose clearly labelled editorial-space sponsorship and off-play display placements only, with ads remaining disabled until configured and approved.
- Challenge: a pitch without a TAM or revenue graph might feel incomplete. Resolution: explicitly present an early product discussion with a milestone-based evidence plan. Founder financing and market research remain diligence work rather than invented numbers.
