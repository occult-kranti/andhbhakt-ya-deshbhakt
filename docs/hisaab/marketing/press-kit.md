# Press and pitch copy

## One sentence

HISAAB DO is a bilingual browser quiz about public money that gives players a source-linked receipt after every answer.

## Short description

HISAAB DO turns questions about Indian public money into a game in English and Hindi. Players can explore files, follow money trails, practise with a labelled bot or play with people they know. Each question includes an explanation and a source link. The visual identity borrows the cream paper, columns and ink of an old newspaper. The humour targets the habit of labelling people for asking questions. Server-refereed competition is an online beta under construction until the release announcement confirms availability.

## Thirty-second founder pitch

“We are building HISAAB DO, a browser game about public money. A player answers a question, gets a receipt with the source, and can bring a friend into the next round. We already have a playable bilingual product. Our next test is whether circles and dependable online duels give people a reason to return and read more receipts. We are looking for partners who can help us run a careful pilot and measure that behaviour. We have not yet supplied traction or revenue figures.”

## Investor meeting opening

“Let me show you one round. The answer matters, but the receipt is the product's editorial promise. The next stage is a small community pilot with server-settled competition, clear timing limits and visible correction routes. We want to establish whether that creates repeat play before making a large acquisition spend.”

## Editorial and correction promise

The source link supports the question's stated fact. Allegations and legal status need precise dates and counterpoints. The game should correct errors visibly. Before a journalist or creator highlights a particular question, recheck the source and the current wording. A source-linked question bank is not evidence that every question was re-fact-checked on this pack's date.
