- **S2 — Maria Rosala, User Control and Freedom**, NN/g, 2020: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-control-and-freedom/>. Primary author summary retrieved. Supports discoverable exits and recovery.
- **S3 — Don Norman, The Design of Everyday Things**, revised edition author page: <https://jnd.org/books/the-design-of-everyday-things-revised-and-expanded-edition/>; author's revised-edition preface: <https://jnd.org/preface-design-of-everyday-things-revised-edition/>. - **S4 — Don Norman, Emotional Design: People and Things**, author essay, 2008: <https://jnd.org/emotional-design-people-and-things/>. Read the author essay discussing appearance, behaviour, and reflective meaning. A visual identity cannot compensate for confusing operation.
- **S5 — Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski, The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination Theory Approach**, 2006: <https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf>. Original paper retrieved. Its competence, autonomy, and relatedness framework informs optional rematches, useful feedback, and voluntary social play; no HISAAB-specific causal claim is implied.

## Loop 1 — Round 2: critique of the moderator's synthesis

The moderator proposed immediate bot resolution using an independent precommitted answer/time; an answered human sees a private personal receipt while their opponent can finish; game settings in every phase; preserved light tokens; and a layered charcoal/violet night edition. This is a review of a proposed contract, not executed implementation evidence.

1. **Accept private immediate feedback with explicit scope.** This refines P1: the answered seat may see *its own* correctness/source immediately, provided the other projection and network events remain redacted. Label the intermediate view “Your answer” and “[name] is still answering”. Do not show a round victory, opponent answer, final score, or advance action yet. Test both guest-first and host-first ordering. A user can voluntarily tell a friend the answer outside the app; private presentation does not establish anti-cheat security.
2. **Separate feedback from earned settlement.** Do not call an estimated XP amount “earned” before the authoritative shared result is recorded. A player who quits during the personal-receipt interval must see the same cancellation promise the implementation actually honours. Already settled rounds and the unresolved round need distinct copy if their persistence differs. Check no XP duplication when private receipt becomes shared receipt.
3. **Protect agency while settings are open.** Native dialog/focus management is appropriate. Suppress answer keyboard shortcuts behind the dialog, keep the timer-running disclosure visible, and ensure a result arriving while the menu is open does not steal focus or strand the dialog. Quit remains a separate confirmation with Resume as the safe focused action. Theme changes must not reset the stopwatch.
4. **Keep bot fairness independent from pacing.** Sample the bot answer and elapsed time before the human response, freeze that sample, and treat fast-forwarding as presentation only. Do not silently replace a slow bot response with the human's elapsed time or make its correctness depend on the chosen answer. Explain the distinction in rules if needed; test repeatability from the same sampled plan.
5. **Accept the night edition as a bounded visual hypothesis.** Light tokens remain exactly unchanged. Repeated wordmark/rule/violet punctuation are recognition cues, not a claim that purple causes trust or retention. Preserve quiet timed cards and one primary continuation action. Validate actual contrast, readable Hindi, and small-screen settings before release.

**Decision:** proceed with the moderator's plan, with the five guards above as review gates. Primary support remains S1–S6; no new behavioural claim or research expansion is needed.

## Loop 2 — Round 2: bounded refinement decision

The moderator accepted P6–P8 and selected: reset stale confirmation when the match settles, preserve the personal announcement, add a passive 17 px `BrandName` above the game format, use the same mark in settings, and make publication pages respect the stored theme preference with operating-system fallback.

Keep its safe control focused, announce the new finished state once, and let Escape close normally. “Back to results” is the clearest settled label, though an unambiguous “Back to game” is not a correctness blocker. The existing recorded match outcome must not be changed by leaving the results.
2. **Announcement ownership:** accept personal/settled receipt preservation. Prevent repeated announcements on every network poll or theme change. This is a screen-reader state-feedback gate, not evidence from assistive-technology participants; inspect the actual live-region update and retain manual screen-reader testing as an empirical follow-up.
3. **Small brand mark:** accept the moderator's bounded tradeoff. Render it from countdown onward so answer-time layout never grows to accommodate branding. At 320 px, verify the score and 44 px settings control remain intact. Use explicit English language on the Latin mark and no interactive behaviour, animation, or second header strip.
4. **Publication theme:** accept matching an explicit saved day/night choice across app and About/Privacy/Contact. Catch storage errors and retain operating-system fallback; never silently override an explicit preference. A short script must set only the theme attribute, not introduce a dependency on account login or analytics.

**Decision:** no expanded scope. Move to the actual candidate and focused transition evidence. S1/S2 support truthful status/control; S4 supports coherent appearance without sacrificing use.
